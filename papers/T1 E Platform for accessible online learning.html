ABSTRACT
This paper presents a web-based platform to improve the educa-
tional experience for students with disabilities in online learning
settings. Developed in collaboration with experts from Centro Es-
pañol de Subtitulado y Audio Descripción (CESyA) and individuals
with disabilities, the platform oers various features to enhance
accessibility. Notably, it enables the creation of live sessions with
live transcription of audio, allowing the inclusion of channels for
sign language interpretation and audio description, and oers a
customizable user interface. Additionally, it automates the gen-
eration of resources such as notes and recordings with subtitles,
streamlining the learning process for all users. The platform has
been rened using user-centred methodologies through iterative
development and user testing to cater to diverse user needs, priori-
tizing accessibility for an eective educational experience tailored
to each student’s requirements.

1 INTRODUCTION
The evolving educational landscape necessitates solutions that
bridge the gap between human and computer interaction, priori-
tizing accessibility and empowering diverse learning styles. This
paper explores a novel web portal designed for online and hybrid
classrooms, focusing on its live session functionality and potential
to enhance accessibility and user interaction.

2 BACKGROUND
Creating an inclusive educational environment poses complex chal-
lenges in both face-to-face and online classrooms, where students
with disabilities encounter barriers hindering their full participa-
tion and academic success. Hearing impairments present signicant
communication obstacles, with traditional and online classrooms
heavily relying on auditory teaching. Visual impairments lead to
diculties accessing course materials and navigating learning envi-
ronments. Meanwhile, students with intellectual disabilities require
personalized teaching and accessible materials. The proposed web
portal prioritizes accessibility, user customization, and adaptive
resources to foster seamless communication and equitable access
to online education for all students.
The right to education is a fundamental human right in Article 26
of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights [
3
]. According to the
Universal Declaration, elementary education should be free for both
boys and girls. Children and adults may improve their abilities and
skills and actively participate in society and all people should have
equal access to education, without any distinction and especially
to vulnerable groups. At this point, accessibility is a key point for
people with disabilities. One of the numerous groups of children in
developing nations who are still not enrolled in elementary school
is disabled kids. But policymakers are increasingly paying greater
attention to their right to education and educational exclusion [
4
].

Governments started taking action in March 2020 to stop the
coronavirus from spreading, stopping schools and switching to
remote learning practically immediately. Approximately 150 na-
tions ultimately shuttered their schools. [
10
]. Therefore, by the
end of March 2020, the majority of institutions, especially upper
secondary and higher education, had switched from traditional in-
class education to online teaching and learning in response to the
COVID-19 educational crisis and to escape the prospect of depriv-
ing their students of education [
11
]. But the COVID-19 pandemic
demonstrated that not only must educational platforms be accessi-
ble, but they must also incorporate accessible videoconferencing
tools. However, current solutions often do not meet the diverse
accessibility needs of students with visual and hearing impairments
in the educational context despite the availability of real-time tran-
scription and video conferencing tools. Therefore, according to
studies on platforms such as Zoom, Teams and Google Meet, there
is still room for improvement in accessibility to meet the needs
of people with disabilities [
6
],[
5
]. For example, according to the
study presented in [
8
], Zoom was preferred to Google Meet and
MS Teams, but none of the tools was fully accessible via keyboard
and screen reader. Other identied constraints include restricted
personalizing and a lack of interaction with the subject material.
In this sense, integrating an accessible videoconferencing solution
with an accessible portal where all aspects may be made available to
the learner is essential. According to [
7
], incorporating multimedia
resources, educational platforms, videoconferencing capabilities,
and supplementary materials is essential to hybrid learning and
presents chances to enhance accessibility.
In [
9
], the authors present a web-based platform that enables the
presentation of subtitles in both single- and multi-screen scenarios,
in a synchronized and customizable manner. This environment
could be used in online learning but it is not integrated into a learn-
ing environment. Also [
2
] combined an online education gaming
environment with a video conferencing system during the pan-
demic for children with special education needs, with a focus on
students having an autism diagnosis. Many articles describe and
discuss how online learning systems and videoconferencing tools
were used. However there is a lack of integration among the learn-
ing management systems, the video conference systems and the
accessibility elements required for the students. Also, it is necessary
to face the problem of simultaneous transmission of the contents
(audio, audio description, captions, sign language interpretation and
presentations) because this is an important issue for both students
and teachers [
1
]. In this paper, we present a platform for online
teaching that combines the elements of an LMS with a video confer-
ence system that incorporates the dierent accessibility elements
(captions, sign language interpretation and audio description) in
an integrated way.
3 PROPOSED SYSTEM
The proposed system presents a comprehensive set of features that
enhance the learning experiences of students with disabilities. In
particular, the system focuses on auditive and visual disabilities. By
addressing the unique needs and challenges faced by individuals
with various disabilities, the system strives to promote accessibility
and engagement in online and in-person classroom environments.
It seeks to ll the gaps in current services by integrating conference
capabilities with educational resources. Moreover, it leverages AI
techniques to generate automatic resources, signicantly enhancing
the learning experience for all users.
The following analysis evaluates the system requirements and
their potential impact on improving learning outcomes for students
with disabilities:
•
User-friendly Interface: An interface with intuitive navi-
gation features is crucial to ensure accessibility and usability
for students with various disabilities. With a clear layout,
consistent design elements, and customizable user interface
to accommodate their diverse needs and preferences.
•
Live Transcription and Captioning: The integration of
live transcription and captioning functionalities addresses
the needs of students with hearing impairments, ensuring
access to spoken content during live sessions. This feature
enhances comprehension and retention for students who rely
on visual or text-based information. Also, channels for non-
automatic sign language interpretation and audio description
can be included for students who need them.
•
Transcription Document: The automatic generation of a
transcription document following each session is a valuable
resource for students with hearing impairments and those
with visual or cognitive disabilities. This allows users to
review the class step by step reading what every user has
said.
•
Notes document: This unique feature combines the tran-
script with synchronized scene changes, mirroring the ow
of the session and oering a visually engaging resource for
diverse learning styles, including visual learners and those
with memory challenges. This document serves as reference
material for reviewing course content at their own pace, fa-
cilitating comprehension and reinforcement of key concepts.
•
Synchronized recordings with automatic subtitles: Record-
ings empower students to revisit lectures at their own pace,
rewinding, pausing, and taking notes alongside the gener-
ated transcript and the auto-generated notes.
•
Compatibility with Assistive Technologies: Compatibil-
ity with assistive technologies such as screen readers and
alternative input devices is essential for ensuring equitable
access to the system for students with disabilities. Compati-
bility testing and adherence to accessibility standards (e.g.
WCAG) are critical to ensuring a barrier-free user experi-
ence.
Overall, our approach to system requirements has been centred
on enhancing the learning experiences of students with disabilities.
Through steadfast prioritization of accessibility, and usability con-
siderations during the design and development phases, the system
aims to empower students with disabilities to participate in the
learning environment. We have followed fundamental design prin-
ciples throughout our process, prioritizing user involvement and
conducting early user testing to gather feedback. Embracing an iter-
ative methodology, we’ve continuously validated our progress with
users, ensuring their needs inform every decision and following
the fundamentals of good design.

3.1 Architecture
Figure 1: System architecture
Figure 1 illustrates the proposed system architecture, designed
to provide a robust and accessible learning platform.
The platform’s frontend, developed using Angular and Google’s
Material design specications, delivers a visually appealing and
user-friendly interface that complies with WCAG 2.1 accessibility
standards. It supports various device formats, including desktop,
tablet, and mobile. Behind the frontend is the Content Manage-
ment System (CMS), which manages data, user authentication, and
administrative functions. It securely stores course materials, user
proles, and session information while providing congurable op-
tions for system administrators. The LiveSessions API acts as the
central control hub for managing live sessions. It handles sched-
uling, authentication, and communication with the media server
for streaming audio and video content. Additionally, it controls
the creation of session resources such as transcription documents
and recordings. Finally, it manages the transcriptions and subtitles
during and after the live session. For the transcriptions it uses cloud
providers, incorporating an in-house trained punctuation model
that enhances readability by automatically adding punctuation
marks to unpunctuated text.
3.2 User interface
The platform’s user interface is meticulously crafted to adhere to
accessibility and design best practices, ensuring intuitive usability.
The UI design undergoes rigorous mocking processes and iterative
renement to enhance the user experience. This approach identies
and addresses potential design aws early, delivering an optimal
user experience. Special attention is given to semantic HTML tags
and logical element hierarchy for intuitive tab navigation, particu-
larly for users relying on screen readers.
The main page design (Figure 2) adopts a standard layout with
a top bar, sidebar, and main content sections, fostering familiarity
and ease of navigation. A search button toggles a modal search bar
for ecient content discovery. The sidebar provides quick access
to sessions and courses, enhancing accessibility and customization.
Clear visual cues and animations contribute to the user-friendly
interface. The main content section oers exible display options,
catering to diverse user preferences with minimal session informa-
tion displayed for easy browsing.

4 VALIDATION
The project uses a user-centred design approach, starting with thor-
ough research into user needs and solution requirements. Various
design solutions were evaluated and rened through iterative feed-
back cycles. Throughout the iterations, the project underwent a
validation process to ensure its eectiveness and alignment with
the needs of users with disabilities. Insights from experts at the
CESyA were integral to shaping the development of the application.
Their expertise in accessibility standards provided invaluable guid-
ance. Furthermore, close collaboration with a blind person allowed
rsthand testing of the application’s accessibility features. This
involvement provided invaluable feedback on the user experience,
leading to enhancements that specically addressed the needs of
this user group.
Furthermore, group usability testing was conducted with in-
dividuals of varying ages and disabilities, simulating real-world
teaching scenarios in a classroom setting. Participants were divided
into groups and given Surface tablets logged into the application.
A researcher guided them through the application’s functionality
using slides displayed on the tablets, followed by a period of free
exploration where participants interacted with the application and
provided feedback through questionnaires.
These tests provided insights into how users interacted with the
application and highlighted areas for improvement. By engaging
with users with diverse disabilities, the system was rened ensuring
it was accessible for all users. Overall, the validation process, which
included input from experts, direct testing with individuals with
disabilities, and group testing with a diverse range of users, played
a crucial role in shaping the application to meet the needs of its
target audience.
5 CONCLUSION
This paper presents a platform for accessible online classes incor-
porating a management system for sessions, subject materials and
live sessions with accessibility elements. The web-based platform
represents a signicant stride towards accessible education, devel-
oped through collaborative eorts with experts and individuals
with disabilities. Prioritizing diverse user needs, the platform’s
automatic resource generation, customizable user interface, and
real-time transcriptions with punctuation bolster accessibility and
streamline the learning process. Presently, the system automatically
generates notes, transcriptions, and subtitled recordings. However,
future iterations will include the generation of session summaries
providing students with easy access to the main points covered.
Additionally, users will have the ability to view video recordings
alongside subtitles and transcriptions for enhanced learning. Fur-
thermore, future work should include additional user testing and a
comprehensive evaluation of the proposed system to ensure its ef-
fectiveness and usability in various educational settings. Ultimately,
the platform is a promising step towards fostering equal opportuni-
ties and cultivating a more inclusive learning environment.
